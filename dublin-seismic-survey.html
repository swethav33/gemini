Dublin Seismic Survey

Dublin will host its first-ever seismic survey in Spring 2027.

Looking beneath the surface

Why carry out a seismic survey?

Seismic surveys use carefully controlled sound or vibration signals and sensitive instruments at the ground surface to investigate the structure of the subsurface. By measuring how seismic waves travel through and reflect from different geological layers, scientists can build a clearer picture of the rocks and sediments below ground.

For geothermal research, this information can help improve our understanding of the depth, geometry and properties of geological formations that may be relevant to the movement and storage of heat and groundwater.

Planned timingSpring 2027

The survey is currently planned for Spring 2027. Further information on the survey area, methods, schedule and any public access arrangements will be added as details are confirmed.

What happens during a seismic survey?

A typical land-based survey uses a line or grid of small sensors called geophones. These instruments record ground motion. A seismic source introduces a controlled vibration, and the recorded signals are processed to help interpret the layers and structures beneath the survey area.

The exact equipment, source type, survey layout and operating arrangements for the Dublin work will be confirmed in advance. This page will be updated with practical information once the final survey plan is available.

What could the results tell us?

  • How geological layers are arranged beneath the survey area.
  • Where major geological boundaries or structures may occur.
  • How the new information can complement existing geological, borehole and geophysical datasets.
  • Where further investigation may help assess geothermal potential.
